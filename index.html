Forecasting is a skill. We're going to measure it.

Prediction@Illinois is a student group forming at UIUC around a narrow claim: the interesting thing about prediction markets is not the bet, it's the number. How a crowd prices an uncertain event, whether that price is calibrated, and what market design makes it better or worse.

We run forecasting tournaments, study live market microstructure with public data, and write the results up. We do not trade and we do not take positions.

01 — Scope

Four things, and nothing else

A first-year club that tries to do everything does nothing. These four share one method: take a published probability, check it against what happened, explain the gap.

Practice

Calibrated forecasting

A semester-long internal tournament scored by Brier and log score. Members forecast, the score is public, and we track whether training moves it. The skill transfers to research, trading and policy alike.

Empirics

Market microstructure

Polymarket and Kalshi publish order books and trade histories over open APIs. We build the pipelines, measure spreads, depth and price impact, and treat fee changes and resolution events as natural experiments.

Theory

Mechanism & market design

LMSR and automated market makers, scoring rules, subsidy design, and the failure mode nobody prices: ambiguous resolution. Where does the oracle break, and what does that cost the price?

Evidence

Forecasts and public policy

Markets versus polls versus expert panels, on the questions where being wrong is expensive. This is where the club's output is most legible to people outside finance — and where faculty collaboration is easiest.

What this group is not

  • Not a trading club. We take no positions, pool no capital, and place no orders. The Illinois Gaming Board's position is that prediction markets are gambling activity under state law; a student group in Illinois that facilitated real-money trading would be on the wrong side of that. Our subject is the data these venues publish, which is public.
  • Not a second Quant@Illinois. They do quantitative finance and HFT recruiting, and they do it well. Our object is the forecast itself — calibration, aggregation, mechanism — which is closer to statistics and market design than to trading.
  • Not a Registered Student Organization yet. We're assembling a founding team first. RSO registration goes through Student Engagement, and we intend to file in Spring 2027.

        Questions we get
        

          
Do I need to know anything about prediction markets?
No. Half the point of the first semester is the tournament, which teaches calibration from scratch. If you can read a probability and argue about it, that's the entry bar.

          
Is this a trading club?
No. We place no orders and hold no positions. We work with the public data these venues publish, which is a different activity with a different legal footing.

          
How is this different from Quant@Illinois?
Quant is quantitative finance and trading recruitment. We're about the forecast as an object of study: how it's formed, whether it's calibrated, and how the mechanism shapes it. Being in both is reasonable.

          
Are you a registered student organization?
Not yet. We're building the founding team first and plan to register through Student Engagement in Spring 2027. Registration requires five officers, a constitution, and a review meeting.

          
Can graduate students join?
Yes. Officer roles are limited to currently enrolled students by university rule, which includes graduate students.
