Weekly scored forecasting, microstructure research on public market data, and mechanism design — with a written result at the end of every semester.

01 — Research

Four instruments, one method

Take a published probability, check it against what happened, explain the gap. Everything below is a different way of doing exactly that.

Practice

Calibrated forecasting

A semester-long tournament scored by Brier and log score. Every forecast is graded by reality; the calibration curve is yours to fix.

Empirics

Market microstructure

Order books and trade histories from public APIs. Spreads, depth, price impact, and the fee changes and resolution events that make natural experiments.

Theory

Mechanism & market design

LMSR and automated market makers, proper scoring rules, subsidy design — and the failure mode nobody prices: an ambiguous resolution source.

Evidence

Forecasts & public policy

Markets versus polls versus expert panels, on questions where being wrong is expensive. The output most legible outside finance, and the easiest place to find faculty.

02 — Program

What a semester looks like

Every member forecasts every week and works on one project. Everything is scored, and everything ends in something written.

01 Weekly

Forecasting round

Twenty questions a semester. Submit probabilities, get a Brier and log score back, and watch your calibration curve move on a public leaderboard.

02 Per question

Resolution review

When a question resolves, we take the crowd's miss apart: who was right, why, and what the market price said at the same moment.

03 Weekly

Reading group

One paper a week on market and mechanism design — scoring rules, LMSR, automated market makers, resolution. One member presents, everyone argues.

04 Semester

Research project

Join a team on one empirical question on public market data. Real pipeline, real analysis, code in the open.

05 End of term

Write-up

Every project ends in a written report with contributors named. It's the artifact you take with you.

03 — Tracks

Pick a market

The four instruments above are the method. Tracks are what you point them at. Everyone forecasts across all of them; your project lives in one. Pick the one whose data you already read for fun.

Elections

Elections & Politics

Polls against prices through the 2026 midterms: margins, turnout, and who calls it first. The oldest prediction-market question, and this semester it's live.

Resolves from certified results, AP calls
Sports

Sports

The deepest markets and the cleanest ground truth. Win probabilities, spreads, and in-game price moves measured against the box score.

Resolves from final scores
Crypto

Crypto

Price-threshold and protocol-event markets where the order book and the chain are both public. The easiest place to do microstructure research end to end.

Resolves from exchange prices, on-chain state
Economics

Economics & Finance

Fed decisions, CPI prints, jobs reports, earnings. Scheduled releases make natural experiments: the price minutes before against the number.

Resolves from BLS, Fed, filings
Tech

Tech & Science

Model releases, product launches, approvals, launches that slip. Fast-resolving questions with strong priors and thin liquidity — good for finding where markets are wrong.

Resolves from official announcements
Weather

Climate & Weather

Daily temperature and precipitation markets that resolve every day — the most calibration reps you can get in a semester. The one track with a published probabilistic baseline to beat: does the market outperform the NWS and ECMWF ensembles?

Resolves from NWS station observations

          Questions we get
          

            
Do I need to know anything about prediction markets?
No. The weekly forecasting round teaches calibration from scratch. If you can read a probability and argue about it, that's the bar.

            
What do members actually work with?
Public data — order books, price histories, and resolution records that prediction-market venues publish over open APIs — plus our own weekly forecasts and scores.

            
What's the time commitment?
About three hours a week: one meeting, one forecasting round, and whatever your project needs that week.

            
Is there a deadline?
Rolling. Applications are reviewed weekly, and you can join mid-semester — you start forecasting from the next round.

            
Can graduate students join?
Yes. Every track is open to graduate students.
